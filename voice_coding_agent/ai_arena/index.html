Color Theory

Color theory is a collection of rules and guidelines designers use to communicate with users through color combinations. It helps you understand how colors interact, mix, and the visual effects of specific color combinations.

1. Primary & Secondary Colors

Primary Colors: Red, Blue, Yellow.
Primary colors are fundamental colors that cannot be created by mixing others.
Example: #FF0000 (Red), #007bff (Blue), #FFD700 (Yellow)
Secondary Colors: Green, Orange, Purple.
Secondary colors are made by mixing two primary colors.
Example: #FFA500 (Orange), #800080 (Purple), #008000 (Green)

2. Complementary Colors

Complementary Colors: Colors opposite each other on the color wheel—provide high contrast.
Example: #FF6347 (Tomato) & #4682B4 (Steel Blue)

3. Analogous Colors

Analogous Colors: Colors adjacent on the color wheel. They match well and create serene designs.
Example: #f39c12 (Yellow Orange), #27ae60 (Green), #2980b9 (Blue)

4. Triadic Colors

Triadic Colors: Three colors evenly spaced around the color wheel—vivid, vibrant contrast.
Example: #e74c3c (Red), #2ecc71 (Green), #3498db (Blue)

5. Color Harmony

Color harmony creates pleasing arrangements and balance. Designers use combinations like complementary, analogous, or triadic schemes for effective color palettes.
